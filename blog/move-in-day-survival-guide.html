Move-In Day Survival Guide: What Nobody Tells First-Years

September 10, 2026 · by Manish HM · 6 min read

Move-in day always looks simple in your head: arrive, unpack, done by lunch. In practice it's usually a few hours longer, a little more chaotic, and full of small decisions nobody warned you about. Here's what actually helps.

Before you even arrive

  • Confirm your room number and block the day before, not the morning of — allocations sometimes change late.
  • Have your ID and admission documents in one folder, not spread across bags. You'll be asked for them more than once.
  • Know your delivery window if anything (furniture, storage, luggage) is being delivered separately — arriving before it does just means standing around.

The paperwork nobody enjoys but everyone needs

Most hostels require a room-allocation form, an ID check, and sometimes a deposit or key handover, before you're actually let into your room. Budget 30–60 minutes for this alone, especially if move-in day is shared with hundreds of other first-years doing the exact same thing at the exact same time.

Meeting your roommate

If you have one, the first conversation worth having isn't about decor — it's about schedules and basics: sleep times, guests, shared vs. personal storage, and how you'll split any shared items. Getting this out of the way on day one avoids a much more awkward version of the same conversation in week three.

Unpacking in the right order

Unpack in this order and the room feels livable faster:

  1. Bed first. You'll want somewhere to sit and a full night's sleep before you do anything else.
  2. Storage second. Get your clothes and bags out of the way before you start on the desk — an open suitcase on the floor makes everything else harder.
  3. Study setup third. Table, chair, lamp, and outlets sorted before you plug anything else in.
  4. Decor and personal touches last. This is the part people rush to first and it's genuinely the least urgent.

Mistakes that make the first week harder

  • Not locating the nearest laundry, mess hall, and washroom on day one. You'll need all three before you need almost anything else.
  • Overpacking clothes, underpacking basics like an extension board, a lock, or a first-aid kit — the boring items you don't think about until you need one.
  • Not testing your outlets and Wi-Fi before your first night, when it's much harder to get help.
The goal for day one isn't a perfect room. It's a bed, a working desk, and knowing where the essentials are — everything else can happen over the first week.

It's okay to feel off for a few days

A new room, a new routine, and often a new city all at once is a lot to adjust to, even when everything goes smoothly. Most of what feels overwhelming on day one is genuinely routine by week two.

One less thing to coordinate on move-in day

Have your room-setup bundle already waiting when you arrive, instead of sourcing furniture the same day you're also doing paperwork.
