Renting vs. Owning: What Actually Makes Sense for Your Hostel Room

August 15, 2026 · by Ashish Bommera · 6 min read

Every new academic year, the same scene plays out in hostel parking lots across the country: parents and students hauling in a mattress topper, a study lamp, a plastic storage box, maybe a folding chair — bought new, days earlier, at full price. Four months later, a chunk of it gets left behind, sold for a fraction of what it cost, or crammed into a taxi that really didn't have room for it.

Nobody sits down and does the math on this. So we did.

The case for owning

Owning makes sense when three things are all true: you'll use the item for years, it's the same item the whole time, and moving it around doesn't cost much. That's your bedroom furniture at your parents' house. It is usually not your hostel room, which you'll occupy for one semester at a time, in a building you don't control, with move-in and move-out dates set by someone else's calendar.

Owning also wins for anything genuinely personal — your own mattress if you have a specific medical need, sentimental items, things you'll actually take with you after graduation. That's a short list for most students.

The case for renting

For everything else — the desk lamp, the extra storage unit, the chair, the mattress topper — renting usually wins on three fronts most people don't compare side by side:

1. The real cost isn't the sticker price

A basic room-setup bundle (mattress topper, study table, lamp, storage, chair) typically costs somewhere between ₹2,000–₹4,000 to buy new, depending on quality. That looks cheaper than renting the same set for a semester — until you count what happens next: reselling it (usually at 30–50% of what you paid, if you can find a buyer at all, and only after real effort), storing it if you can't sell it, or replacing it every year because dorm-grade furniture doesn't survive multiple house moves.

Renting collapses all of that into one number you know upfront, and it's usually lower than owning-and-reselling once you actually account for the resale loss.

2. Moving day gets a lot easier

Most students move at least twice a year — in for the semester, out for the break, sometimes into a different room entirely the next term. Every one of those moves costs time, effort, and usually a vehicle you have to arrange. Furniture you own has to go with you or get stored somewhere. Furniture you rent just gets collected by whoever delivered it.

3. Condition risk isn't yours to carry

Own a chair, and a wobbly leg or a stained mattress topper is now your problem to fix, replace, or explain to whoever you're trying to sell it to. Rent the same thing, and normal wear is already priced in — you're not the one holding the depreciation.

The furniture itself doesn't care who owns it. What changes is who's stuck moving it, storing it, and eating the loss when it's time to leave.

A simple way to decide

Ask one question about anything you're about to buy for your room: will I still want this exact item in the same condition a year from now, in a different room? If yes, buying is reasonable. If the honest answer is "probably not" — which covers most hostel-grade furniture — renting is almost always the better call, both financially and in terms of your own time.
