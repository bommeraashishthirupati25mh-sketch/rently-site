Renting vs. Owning

The Hidden Costs of Owning Furniture as a Student

August 22, 2026 · by Sai Krishna Gopal Veluri · 5 min read

"Just buy it, it's cheaper in the long run" is one of the most repeated pieces of advice students get before move-in day. It's also usually wrong, because it only counts one number: the price tag. Here's what actually gets left out of that calculation.

1. The resale tax

Almost nothing you buy for a hostel room retains its value. Sell a mattress topper, a study lamp, or a plastic storage unit after a semester of use, and you're realistically looking at 30–50% of what you paid — if you find a buyer at all before you have to move out. Most students don't have the time to run a resale process in the same week they're packing for exams and a flight home, so a lot of this furniture doesn't get sold. It gets left in a hallway, given away for nothing, or thrown out.

2. The storage tax

If you don't sell it and you're coming back next semester, it has to go somewhere over the break. That's either a storage unit you now have to pay for and coordinate, or space in your family home for something you'll use for four more months, eight states away.

3. The transport tax

Furniture doesn't move itself. Getting a table and a storage unit from a shop to your hostel room, and later out again, means arranging a vehicle, physically carrying it up stairs, and doing it all over again at the next move. This is real time and real money that a delivered-and-collected rental simply removes from your plate.

4. The condition tax

Own something, and any damage, wear, or a wobbly leg is now entirely your problem — to live with, to fix, or to explain away when you're trying to sell it. It also means every scrape and stain is your loss to absorb, not something already priced into what you paid.

5. The replacement tax

Budget hostel-grade furniture isn't built for repeated moving. A mattress topper or a foldable table that survives one dorm room often doesn't survive being packed into a taxi, unpacked, used again, and packed away a second time. A lot of students end up rebuying the same category of item every year — which means they've now paid the full price more than once for something they never actually kept long-term.

Add it up — sticker price, minus what you actually got back on resale, plus storage, plus transport, plus the fact that half of it broke before you could sell it — and "buying is cheaper" stops being true for most short-term hostel furniture.

What doesn't have these costs

The items worth owning are the ones where none of this applies: things you'll keep for years, that travel with you unchanged, where resale was never part of the plan. For a hostel room you'll occupy for one semester at a time, that's a short list — and it's usually not the desk lamp.
